For Buyers

The Buyer’s Journey

Buying a home is one of the largest purchases most people ever make. Here is the whole process, start to finish, so nothing about it catches you by surprise.

The Process

Ten steps to a new home

Every purchase moves through the same ten stages. Some take an afternoon, some take weeks — but knowing which one you are standing in is most of what makes the process feel manageable.

  1. 01

    Find the right agent

    The seller already has someone in their corner — a listing agent whose job is to get them top dollar. A buyer’s agent owes that same duty to you. Interview more than one, and ask about fees, contract length, and how many clients they are carrying right now.

  2. 02

    Prepare your finances

    Pull your credit report and flag any errors. Gather pay stubs, bank statements and past tax returns. Then decide what monthly payment you are actually comfortable with — remembering that taxes, insurance and any HOA dues sit on top of the mortgage itself.

  3. 03

    Get pre-approved

    Pre-qualified and pre-approved are not the same thing. Pre-approval means a lender has verified your income, assets, debt and credit and committed to a specific amount. It is what makes your offer competitive. Ask whether you qualify for a VA, FHA or state program while you are at it.

  4. 04

    Start home shopping

    Write down what the house has to have — bedrooms, bathrooms, kitchen size, storage, yard. Then think about everything around it: the commute, the shopping, how well the neighbouring homes are kept, how much traffic runs down the street. Spend real time in each area before you narrow down.

  5. 05

    Make an offer

    We assess what the property is actually worth in today’s market with a comparative market analysis, then build an offer around it. In a seller’s market the rule of thumb is to start strong rather than leave room to climb.

  6. 06

    Negotiate

    An offer is accepted, declined, or countered. A counter means the seller wants different terms — you can accept, decline, or counter back. This can go back and forth as many rounds as it needs to, until you agree or one side walks. When everyone agrees, you are under contract.

  7. 07

    Order the inspection

    During the option period a licensed inspector goes through the house and hands back a list of findings. This is your leverage: what turns up here is what you negotiate on next, and it is also what tells you whether to keep going at all.

  8. 08

    The appraisal

    If you are financing, your lender orders an appraisal before the sale can close. You do not want it to come in below the price you negotiated — if it does, the gap has to be renegotiated or covered in cash. I work the appraisal deliberately to support the agreed value.

  9. 09

    Plan your move

    The month between contract and keys fills up faster than people expect — movers, utilities, insurance quotes, title work, address changes. There is a week-by-week schedule further down this page.

  10. 10

    Closing day

    You sign the ownership and insurance paperwork and you get the keys. Closing typically lands three to five weeks after you go under contract.

Before You Start

What you’ll need in cash

Four separate costs, paid at different moments. Lenders generally advise against stretching past three to five times your annual household income.

Down payment 3.5–20%

Of the purchase price, depending on your loan type. Some programs go lower.

Earnest money Varies

A good-faith deposit that signals you are serious. Back out without cause and you lose it.

Closing costs 2–5%

Of the loan amount, due at the closing table. Sometimes negotiable as a seller credit.

Home inspection $350–$625

Typical Dallas–Fort Worth range, scaling with square footage and the age of the home.

Figures are general guidance for the Dallas–Fort Worth market, not a quote. Your lender and title company will give you exact numbers for your transaction.

Winning The Home

Five ways to strengthen an offer

In a competitive market the highest number does not automatically win. These are the levers that move a seller.

Competitive offer

Start strong. We price the offer off a real comparative market analysis rather than off the asking price.

Earnest money

Consider doubling what the seller asks for, or what is customary locally. It is the clearest signal of intent you can send.

The gift of time

Ask the sellers what their ideal closing date is, then work to it. Flexibility on timing often beats money on the table.

Make a connection

Tell them why you love the house. Sellers reviewing a stack of offers remember the family who wrote about their kitchen.

Limit contingencies

Contingencies protect you but slow the seller down. The National Association of Realtors finds the offer with the fewest is often the most attractive — so we keep the ones that matter and drop the rest.

After The Inspection

Five ways forward

If the report turns up problems, get a contractor’s estimate for the repairs first. That number is the leverage — and I handle the negotiation from there.

  • Ask the seller to make the repairs
  • Ask for credits toward your closing costs
  • Ask for a price reduction to cover them
  • Back out — within the option period
  • Move forward with the deal as written

Closing Day

The final walk-through

We walk the house within 24 hours of closing — about an hour — to confirm its condition and that any agreed repairs actually got done.

  • Every appliance runs
  • Every faucet runs, and nothing leaks
  • Garage doors open and close on the opener
  • Toilets flush; disposal and exhaust fans work
  • Bring a government photo ID and proof of wire or a cashier’s check

Scheduling Your Move

The last four weeks

Four weeks out

Book and pack

Give 30 days’ notice if you are renting. Book the movers or the truck, buy packing materials, and start packing.

Three weeks out

Sort and record

Go through every drawer and closet — donate or sell what you will not want in the new house. Collect medical records and inventory anything valuable.

Two weeks out

Switch it over

Contact water, electric and cable. Change your mailing address and subscriptions. Stop buying groceries you will only have to move.

One week out

Finish and confirm

Finish packing and clean. Set aside a box of essentials for the first few nights. Confirm the delivery date, and write down the new address and your cell number for the movers.
